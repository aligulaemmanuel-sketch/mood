First aid basics · Huduma ya kwanza

+

First check: DRABC · Kagua kwanza

  1. D
    Danger · Hatari

    Check traffic, fire, electricity and other hazards. Approach only if safe.

  2. R
    Response · Mwitikio

    Speak loudly and gently tap their shoulders. Do not shake someone with a possible injury.

  3. A
    Airway · Njia ya hewa

    Look for an open airway. Open it only if you know how or the emergency dispatcher instructs you.

  4. B
    Breathing · Kupumua

    Check for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Gasping is not normal breathing.

  5. C
    Circulation / severe bleeding · Damu nyingi

    Look for dangerous bleeding and press firmly on it. Do not try to check a pulse.

If someone is unresponsive, not breathing normally, has life-threatening bleeding, or is in immediate danger: call 999 or 112 now, put the phone on speaker, and follow the dispatcher.

Illustrated first-aid guides · Miongozo ya huduma ya kwanza

Tap a picture to open its steps. These are brief educational reminders—not a substitute for emergency dispatch or practical first-aid training.

Unresponsive / CPRAsiyeitikia / CPR+
  1. Call 999/112; ask someone to get an AED. Put the phone on speaker.
  2. If unresponsive and not breathing normally (or only gasping), start chest compressions in the centre of the chest.
  3. For an adult, push hard and fast: 100–120 compressions/minute, about 5–6 cm deep; let the chest rise between pushes.
  4. If trained and willing, use 30 compressions to 2 breaths. Otherwise do continuous compressions. Use an AED as soon as available and follow its prompts.

Piga 999/112 na uombe AED. Ikiwa mtu haitikii na hapumui kawaida, anza kubonyeza katikati ya kifua kwa nguvu na haraka. Kwa mtu mzima: mara 100–120 kwa dakika, kina cha takriban sentimita 5–6. Fuata maelekezo ya mhudumu. Mwongozo wa watoto wachanga ni tofauti.

If they are breathing normally, do not do CPR; monitor breathing and follow the dispatcher. CPR guidance for infants and children differs—follow dispatcher instructions and your training.

Severe bleedingDamu nyingi+
  1. Call 999/112 for heavy, spurting or uncontrolled bleeding. Use gloves or a barrier if available.
  2. Press firmly and continuously on the wound with clean gauze or cloth.
  3. If blood comes through, keep the first dressing in place and add more on top. Do not keep lifting it to check.
  4. Use a commercial tourniquet only for life-threatening limb bleeding if trained; follow its instructions and tell responders when it was applied.

Piga 999/112 ikiwa damu ni nyingi au haikomi. Bonyeza jeraha kwa nguvu bila kuachia kwa kitambaa safi. Damu ikipenya, ongeza vitambaa juu ya cha kwanza; usikiondoe. Tumia tourniquet ya dukani tu ikiwa umefunzwa.

Do not remove embedded objects or apply a tourniquet made from a belt or rope.

ChokingKukabwa+

Adult or child over 1 year: If they can cough or speak, encourage coughing. If they cannot breathe, speak or cough, call 999/112. Lean them forward; give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades. If not cleared, give up to 5 abdominal thrusts. Alternate 5 and 5.

Infant under 1 year: Call for help. Give up to 5 back blows, then up to 5 chest thrusts. Do not use abdominal thrusts on an infant.

Ikiwa anaweza kukohoa, mhimize kukohoa. Akishindwa kupumua au kuongea, piga 999/112. Mtoto mkubwa/mtu mzima: hadi mapigo 5 mgongoni, kisha misukumo 5 tumboni; badilisha. Mtoto mchanga chini ya mwaka 1: mapigo 5 mgongoni na misukumo 5 kifuani—si tumboni.

If they become unresponsive, lower them safely, call emergency services, and start CPR following dispatcher guidance. Do not do a blind finger sweep.

BurnsKuungua+
  1. Stop the burning and cool the area under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 minutes. Keep the rest of the person warm.
  2. Remove jewellery and loose clothing near the burn; do not pull away anything stuck to skin.
  3. Cover loosely with clean non-fluffy material or cling film laid over the burn—not wrapped around a limb.
  4. Get urgent care for large, deep, facial, hand, genital, major joint, electrical or chemical burns; call 999/112 for severe injury.

Poza sehemu iliyoungua kwa maji yanayotiririka kwa dakika 20. Ondoa mapambo na nguo zisizoganda. Funika kwa kitambaa safi bila kubana. Tafuta huduma haraka kwa jeraha kubwa, la kina, usoni, mkononi, sehemu za siri, umeme au kemikali.

Do not use ice, butter, oils, toothpaste or creams; do not burst blisters. For a chemical burn, protect yourself and rinse with plenty of clean water while getting urgent advice.

Severe allergyMzio mkali+
  1. Call 999/112 immediately for breathing difficulty, tongue/throat swelling, collapse or rapidly worsening symptoms.
  2. Help the person use their own prescribed adrenaline auto-injector as directed; inject into the outer mid-thigh through clothing if the device instructions permit.
  3. Keep them lying flat with legs raised if this does not worsen breathing. If breathing is difficult, allow them to sit with legs out; do not let them stand or walk.
  4. If symptoms persist, a second prescribed auto-injector may be used after 5 minutes if available. Follow the dispatcher and device instructions.

Piga 999/112 mara moja kwa shida ya kupumua, koo/ulimi kuvimba au kuzimia. Msaidie kutumia sindano yake ya adrenaline kulingana na maelekezo. Mweke amelala ikiwa anaweza kupumua; asisimame wala kutembea. Dalili zikiendelea baada ya dakika 5, fuata maelekezo ya mhudumu na kifaa kuhusu dozi nyingine.

Symptoms can return. Emergency medical assessment is needed even if they improve.

Fracture / sprainKuvunjika / kuteguka+
  1. Keep the person still and support the injured part in the position found. Do not straighten it.
  2. For an open wound, cover it with a clean dressing; do not push exposed bone back in.
  3. For a closed sprain, a wrapped cold pack for up to 20 minutes may help. Stop if skin becomes numb or painful.
  4. Get urgent care for deformity, severe pain, numbness, an open fracture, or inability to use the limb.

Msaidie mtu asisogee na tegemeza kiungo kilivyo—usikinyoshe. Funika jeraha wazi kwa kitambaa safi; usirudishe mfupa ndani. Kwa kuteguka, weka kitu baridi kilichofungwa kwa kitambaa hadi dakika 20.

Do not force movement or apply ice directly to skin. Call 999/112 for major trauma or immediate danger.

ShockMshtuko wa mwili+
  1. Call 999/112. Shock may follow serious injury or illness; signs can include pale, cold, clammy skin, fast breathing or confusion.
  2. Help them lie down if comfortable and safe. Do not move them if a spine, hip or leg injury is possible unless there is immediate danger.
  3. Keep them warm and still; loosen tight clothing. Do not give food or drink.
  4. Watch breathing and responsiveness. If they become unresponsive and are not breathing normally, start CPR following dispatcher guidance.

Piga 999/112. Dalili zinaweza kuwa ngozi kupauka, kuwa baridi/jasho, kupumua haraka au kuchanganyikiwa. Mweke alale ikiwa ni salama; usimhamishe ikiwa kuna jeraha la mgongo, nyonga au mguu. Mfunike; usimpe chakula wala kinywaji.

Do not routinely raise the legs after trauma or if it worsens pain or breathing.

SnakebiteKuumwa na nyoka+
  1. Move away from the snake. Keep the person calm and as still as possible; call 999/112 or an ambulance provider and arrange urgent transport to a health facility.
  2. Keep the bitten limb still and supported. Remove rings, watches or tight items before swelling.
  3. Note the time of the bite. If safe, remember the snake’s appearance from a distance; do not try to catch or kill it.

Ondokeni karibu na nyoka. Mtulize mtu na apunguze mwendo; piga 999/112 au ambulensi na tafuteni huduma haraka. Tuliza kiungo kilichoumwa na ondoa pete au vitu vinavyobana kabla hakijavimba.

Do not cut or suck the wound, apply ice or a tourniquet, or delay transport while searching for the snake.

Fire responseUsalama wakati wa moto+
  1. Rescue: Help people in immediate danger only if you can do so without entering smoke or fire. Do not delay your own escape.
  2. Alarm: Activate the nearest fire alarm if safe. From a safe place call 999 or 112; give the building, floor, nearest road/landmark, and whether anyone is trapped.
  3. Contain: Close doors behind you if this will not delay escape. Do not lock them.
  4. Evacuate: Use marked stairs, never a lift. Stay low under smoke, go to the agreed assembly point, and do not re-enter.

Small fire only: Use an appropriate, maintained extinguisher only if you are trained, the fire is still small, and a clear exit is behind you. If it does not go out immediately, leave and close the door.

PASS: Pull the pin, Aim at the base, Squeeze the handle, Sweep across the base. Follow the extinguisher label and building plan; extinguisher classes vary. Never use water on electrical equipment, fuel, or burning cooking oil.

If trapped: Stay in a room with the door closed, block smoke gaps if possible, call 999/112 and state your exact room/floor. Signal from a window only if safe; do not open it into smoke or flames.

Moto ukitokea: waokoe walio hatarini ikiwa ni salama; toa tahadhari na piga 999/112 ukiwa mahali salama. Toka kwa ngazi, usitumie lifti, kaa chini chini ya moshi, na usirudi ndani. Zima moto mdogo tu ikiwa umefunzwa na njia ya kutoka iko wazi.

Do not fight a spreading fire, breathe smoke, or re-enter for belongings. For a cooking-oil fire, never pour water on it. Follow the marked exit plan and local fire authority instructions.

Road crash / handoverAjali ya barabarani / makabidhiano+
  1. Watch for traffic, fire, fuel, unstable vehicles and electricity. Do not enter danger; call 999/112. Move a casualty only if staying puts them in immediate danger.
  2. Give the dispatcher the exact location or nearest landmark, callback number, number of injured people and visible hazards.
  3. At handover, report what happened, when it happened, changes in breathing/responsiveness, bleeding, and first aid given. Mention known allergies or medicines if known.

Jihadhari na magari, moto, mafuta na nyaya za umeme. Usijiweke hatarini; piga 999/112. Mpe mhudumu mahali/alama ya karibu, nambari ya kurudi, idadi ya waliojeruhiwa na hatari zilizopo. Waeleze wahudumu kilichotokea na msaada uliotoa.

Do not remove a helmet or move someone with a possible neck/back injury unless there is immediate danger or a dispatcher directs you.
